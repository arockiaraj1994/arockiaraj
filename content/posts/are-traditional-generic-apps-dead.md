Most of the software we use is built for everyone. One app, many customers. For a while now I
have been asking myself: are these generic apps already dead, or are they slowly going to die?

## How we have always built software

The basic idea in our industry is simple. Understand the business problem, then build software
that many people can reuse.

Think about shops. There are lots of shops, and every one of them needs billing software and
tax software. Nobody builds a new billing app for each shop. We build one app and sell it to many
shops.

## Users pay for it

The problem is that one app cannot fit every shop perfectly.

So the users adjust. They learn to live with a few things they don't need. Some of them even
change the way they run their business so it matches the app, when it should be the other way
around.

We did it this way because building a separate app for every customer, and then looking after all
of those apps, was very hard. One app for everyone was the only practical choice.

## Building is not hard anymore

Today, building an app is not the problem.

The hard part is what comes after. Who fixes the bugs? Who keeps it updated? Who picks up the
phone when a customer is stuck? Running the app after it goes live is where the real work is
now, not writing the code.

## Why build specific apps at all?

If building is easy, should we build an app for each customer? I think yes.

Everyone wants things their own way in daily life. Look at the home screen on anyone's phone.
Give the same phone to a few people and soon every home screen looks different. We like setting
things up the way that suits us, and there are many other examples like this.

When an app is built for one business or one person, it can fit the way they work. It can also
have features that a generic app would never add, because only that one customer needs them.

## What about maintenance?

One more point. A generic app needs more maintenance, not less. It has to handle settings and
options for every customer in one place, and a fix for one customer can break something for
another.

A specific app only has what that one customer needs. So there is less to maintain.

## So, dead or not?

Generic apps became the normal way because building an app for each customer cost too much. That
reason is going away. What is left is the work of running and supporting the app.

I don't think generic apps are dead yet. But the main reason we built them is getting weaker, and
I am not sure how long they will last.
